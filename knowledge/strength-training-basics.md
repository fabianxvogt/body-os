# Strength training basics

For beginners and people returning after months or years off.

## The core idea: progressive overload

Muscles and strength adapt to demands that are slightly harder than what they are used to. Over
weeks, add a little: one more rep, a bit more weight, or one more set. [STRONG] (1, 2)
Consistency over months matters far more than the "perfect" programme. [STRONG] (1)

## A simple starting structure

- **Full body, 3× per week** (e.g. Mon/Wed/Fri) suits starters and re-starters: each muscle is
  trained ~3× weekly, and a missed day costs little. [MODERATE] (frequency per se matters less
  than weekly volume, but full-body is practical) (1, 3)
- Each session: 4–6 exercises covering **squat or leg press, hinge (Romanian deadlift, hip
  thrust), horizontal push (bench, push-up), horizontal pull (row), vertical push/pull
  (overhead press, lat pulldown/pull-up)**, plus optional core or arms. [OPINION] standard practice
- 45–60 minutes including warm-up is enough. [OPINION]

## Sets, reps and effort

| Goal | Reps per set | Sets | Label |
|---|---|---|---|
| Muscle size | Anywhere ~6–30 works if sets end close to failure; 6–15 is practical | ~10+ hard sets per muscle per week | [STRONG] (1, 3, 4) |
| Maximal strength | ~1–6 heavy reps (≥ ~80 % of max) | 2–3+ per exercise | [STRONG] (1) |
| Starting out | 8–12 reps | 2–3 per exercise | [OPINION] |

Even 1 set per exercise twice a week gives real benefit; more volume gives more growth up to a
point. [STRONG] (1, 3)

## RPE and RIR — measuring effort

- **RIR (reps in reserve):** how many more reps you could have done with good form.
  RIR 2 = "two left in the tank".
- **RPE 1–10:** RPE 8 ≈ RIR 2, RPE 10 = nothing left.
- Most working sets at **RIR 1–3** give near-maximal growth with lower injury and fatigue risk;
  training to absolute failure every set is not necessary. [MODERATE] (5)
- Beginners underestimate how many reps they have left; the estimate gets better with practice.
  [MODERATE] (5)

## Double progression (easy to follow and to log)

1. Pick a rep range, e.g. 8–12, and a weight you can do for 8 at RIR ~2.
2. Each session, try to add reps across all sets.
3. When **all sets reach 12**, raise the weight by the smallest step (+2.5 kg lower body,
   +1–2.5 kg upper body) and drop back to ~8 reps.
4. If reps fall for two sessions in a row, check sleep, food and stress before blaming the plan.
[OPINION] widely used coaching method; consistent with overload principle [STRONG]

## Deloads

- A lighter week (about half the sets, or lighter weights) every 4–8 weeks, or when performance
  drops and joints ache, is common practice. [OPINION] (6)
- Strong evidence for fixed deload schedules is lacking; surveys show coaches use them mainly to
  manage fatigue. For beginners, life interruptions often act as natural deloads. [WEAK] (6)

## Returning after years off ("muscle memory")

- People who trained before usually **regain strength and size faster** than they built it the
  first time. Skill and coordination come back quickly. [MODERATE]
- Short breaks (~3 weeks) cost little muscle. [MODERATE] (7)
- Cell level: extra myonuclei gained in training **are kept** during months of detraining in a
  2024 human study, and a small study found epigenetic "memory" — but whether this makes regrowth
  faster in humans is still unclear. [WEAK] (8, 9, 10)
- Practical re-entry: start at ~50–60 % of old working weights, 2 sets per exercise, RIR 3–4 for
  the first 2 weeks. Expect strong soreness after the first sessions; it fades. [OPINION]

## Technique and safety

- Learn movements with light weights, film a set occasionally, or book a session with a coach.
- Sharp or joint pain (not muscle burn) → stop that exercise, swap it, reassess. [OPINION]
- Resistance training has a low injury rate compared with many sports. [MODERATE]

## How to log a session

One line per exercise is enough: `exercise · weight × reps per set · RIR of last set`.
Example: `Squat 60 kg × 10, 10, 9 · RIR 2`. Note pain, sleep or illness if relevant.

## How body-os uses this

- **Tracked:** sessions, exercises, weight × reps, RIR, missed sessions, pain notes.
- **Computed:** weekly hard sets per muscle group, best-set trends, estimated progress.
- **Weekly review question:** "Did I train as planned, and did load or reps go up on the main
  lifts? If not, what got in the way (sleep, food, schedule)?"

## Not medical advice — see a doctor if …

- you have chest pain, unusual breathlessness, dizziness or palpitations during exercise;
- **dark (cola-coloured) urine with severe muscle pain and swelling** after a hard session —
  possible rhabdomyolysis, seek care the same day;
- joint pain persists > 2 weeks, or a pop/sudden pain with swelling occurs;
- you have a heart condition, high blood pressure or other illness before starting heavy training.
