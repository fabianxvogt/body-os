# Endurance: easy aerobic training ("zone 2") and VO2max

## Why aerobic fitness matters

- **VO2max** (cardiorespiratory fitness, CRF) is the maximum amount of oxygen your body can use.
  It is one of the strongest predictors of long-term survival in observational studies.
  Each 1-MET (≈3.5 ml/kg/min) higher fitness is linked to roughly **11–17 % lower all-cause
  mortality**; high vs low fitness ≈ half the risk. [STRONG for association; certainty of
  individual studies low–moderate] (1, 2)
- Association is not proof that raising VO2max alone causes the benefit, but training that raises
  it also improves blood pressure, glucose control and mood. [MODERATE]
- WHO recommends **150–300 min/week of moderate** or **75–150 min/week of vigorous** activity,
  plus muscle strengthening on ≥ 2 days. [STRONG] (3)

## What "zone 2" means

- Popular term for steady exercise just **below the first lactate/ventilatory threshold**:
  you breathe deeper but can still speak in full sentences. [MODERATE for the definition]
- Heart-rate zones from formulas (e.g. 60–70 % of max HR) only roughly match each person's
  threshold. [MODERATE]
- **Talk test:** if you can talk comfortably in sentences, you are usually below the first
  threshold; if speech becomes choppy, you are above it. Validity is supported, protocols vary.
  [MODERATE] (4)

## Is zone 2 special?

- Easy aerobic work is safe, repeatable, easy to recover from and builds a base. [STRONG]
- Claims that zone 2 is the *optimal* intensity for mitochondria or fat burning mainly come from
  elite athletes training 10+ hours/week. For people with limited time, **higher intensities
  (intervals) raise VO2max at least as well or better**. [MODERATE] (5, 6)
- A practical mix for most: mostly easy sessions + **one interval session per week**
  (e.g. 4 × 4 min hard, 3 min easy). [OPINION] consistent with (5, 6)

## Does cardio kill gains? (interference effect)

- Meta-analysis of 43 studies: adding aerobic training to strength training did **not reduce
  muscle growth or maximal strength**. [STRONG] (7)
- It slightly reduced **explosive strength** (jumping, sprinting), mainly when both were done in
  the same session; separating by ≥ 3 h removed the effect. [MODERATE] (7)
- Older data suggested running interferes more than cycling; the newer meta-analysis found no
  clear difference. [WEAK] (7, 8)
- Practical: keep most cardio easy, do it on separate days or after lifting, and eat enough. When
  struggling to gain weight, each cardio session raises the food you need. [OPINION]

## Simple field tests you can repeat

| Test | How | What it shows | Label |
|---|---|---|---|
| Resting HR | On waking, lying, 1 min, 3 mornings → average | Falls as fitness rises; also rises with illness, alcohol, poor sleep | [MODERATE] (9) |
| UKK 2-km walk test | Walk 2 km on flat ground as fast as possible (no running); record time and HR at finish; calculator gives fitness index / VO2max estimate | Moderate accuracy at group level, ± several ml/kg/min individually; not for very fit people | [MODERATE] (10) |
| Cooper 12-min run | Cover maximum distance in 12 min on a track | Estimates VO2max; needs pacing experience and some fitness | [MODERATE] |
| Stair test | Climb 4 flights (~60 steps) briskly without stopping or running | < ~45 s ≈ good exercise capacity, ≥ 90 s ≈ low; from a small study of heart patients | [WEAK] (11) |
| Wearable VO2max estimate | From outdoor runs/walks with HR | Trend is more useful than the absolute number; error ~5–15 % | [MODERATE] |

### Making tests comparable

- Same route/track, same time of day, similar weather, same shoes, same device.
- No hard training the day before, no caffeine/nicotine/cannabis 2–3 h before if possible
  (all raise or alter HR). [OPINION]
- Repeat every **6–8 weeks**; compare trend, not single results. [OPINION]
- Note anything unusual (cold, bad sleep, heat) in the test log.

## How body-os uses this

- **Tracked:** cardio sessions (type, minutes, avg HR, talk-test feel), resting HR, field test
  results with conditions, wearable VO2max estimate, weekly minutes vs WHO target.
- **Weekly review question:** "Did I reach ~150 min of moderate activity (or equivalent) without
  hurting my lifting, and is resting HR / test performance trending in the right direction?"

## Not medical advice — see a doctor if …

- chest pain, pressure or tightness during exertion; fainting or near-fainting;
- breathlessness out of proportion to effort, or wheezing that does not settle;
- palpitations / irregular heartbeat, or resting HR persistently > 100 or unexpectedly < 40
  without endurance training;
- you smoke and notice exercise tolerance falling — ask about a lung function test.
