# Body metrics: what to track and why

Track few things, measured the same way every time. Trends beat single numbers. [OPINION]

## Everyday metrics

| Metric | How | Why it is worth it | Label |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Weight** | Morning, after toilet, before food/drink, same scale; use the **7-day average** | Daily swings of 0.5–2 kg are water/food; the weekly average shows real change | [MODERATE] |
| **Waist** | Tape midway between lowest rib and hip bone, after a normal breath out, relaxed | Tracks belly fat better than weight; European men ≥ 94 cm / women ≥ 80 cm = increased risk, ≥ 102 / ≥ 88 cm = high risk | [STRONG] (1, 2) |
| Other circumferences (upper arm, chest, thigh) | Same spot, relaxed, every 2–4 weeks | Shows where gains go during muscle building | [OPINION] |
| **Resting heart rate** | On waking, lying down, 1 min; wearable night value is OK if consistent | Lower with fitness; each +10 bpm is linked to higher mortality; temporary rises flag illness, alcohol, poor sleep | [MODERATE] (3) |
| **Steps** | Phone or watch daily average | Mortality benefit rises up to ~7,000–10,000 steps/day, then flattens | [MODERATE] (4, 5) |
| **Sleep** | Duration and regularity (see [sleep.md](sleep.md)) | Strong link to recovery, appetite, mood | [STRONG] |
| Subjective ratings | Energy, mood, stress, soreness, 1–5 daily | Cheap early-warning signals; useful next to substance changes | [OPINION] |

## Heart rate variability (HRV) — useful but noisy

- HRV (often rMSSD) reflects nervous-system balance; higher is usually "more recovered". [MODERATE] (6)
- It varies a lot day to day and between people; compare only with **your own 7-day rolling
  average**, measured the same way (overnight or a morning reading). [MODERATE] (6)
- Alcohol, illness, late meals, stress, cannabis and nicotine changes all shift it. Values from
  different devices are not comparable. Don't change a training plan on one low reading. [OPINION]

## Blood pressure at home

Protocol (ESH; similar in AHA/ACC): [STRONG] (7, 8, 9)
- Validated **upper-arm** device (not wrist, not smartwatch); **cuff size to arm circumference**
  (too small a cuff reads high).
- Sit 5 min, back supported, feet flat, arm resting at heart level, no talking; no caffeine,
  nicotine or exercise 30 min before; empty bladder.
- **2 readings, 1 min apart, morning (before meds/breakfast) and evening, for 7 days** (minimum 3);
  discard day 1; average the rest.
- Repeat the 7-day series every few months, or as your doctor advises.

| Value | Meaning | Label |
|---|---|---|
| Office < 120/70 | Non-elevated (ESC 2024) | [STRONG] (9) |
| Office 120–139 / 70–89 | "Elevated" (ESC 2024) — lifestyle measures, recheck; home values run ~5 mmHg lower | [STRONG] category (9); [MODERATE] home offset |
| **Home average ≥ 135/85** | Hypertension range (ESH/ESC) → see a doctor to confirm; AHA/ACC 2025 use lower cut-offs (stage 1 from 130/80) | [STRONG] (7, 8, 9) |
| ≥ 180/110, or any high reading with chest pain, severe headache, vision or speech problems | **Emergency (112)** | [STRONG] |

Nicotine and cannabis can raise heart rate and blood pressure for a while after use — note use
before measuring. [MODERATE]

## Lab values that matter for a young adult

In Germany, statutory insurance covers a **one-time check-up between 18 and 34**; blood tests are
included only with risk factors (e.g. family history, overweight, high blood pressure); from 35
every 3 years with lipids and glucose. [STRONG — G-BA] (10)

Worth knowing at least once, especially if you smoke or have risk factors: [MODERATE]
- **Lipids** (LDL, HDL, triglycerides) and **glucose / HbA1c**.
- **Blood count** (haemoglobin, white cells), **liver enzymes** (alcohol, medication), **kidney**
  (creatinine; note high protein intake or creatine supplements can raise creatinine slightly).
- Ferritin, TSH, vitamin D: only with symptoms or a reason — ask your doctor.

**Slightly raised white blood cell count in smokers:** smoking commonly raises white cells (mainly
neutrophils) by roughly 10–25 %; counts fall within about a year after quitting. [MODERATE] (11)
A raised count can also mean infection, inflammation or — rarely — a blood disorder, so **don't
self-explain it: show it to your doctor**, mention smoking, and ask whether to recheck. [OPINION]

## Lung function (spirometry) for smokers

- Screening people **without symptoms** is not recommended (USPSTF); but anyone who smokes and has
  **cough, phlegm, wheeze or breathlessness** should get spirometry (GOLD case-finding). [STRONG] (12, 13)
- A baseline test can be useful after many pack-years even without symptoms — discuss with a GP.
  [OPINION] (13)

## How body-os uses this

- **Tracked:** weight, waist and circumferences, resting HR, HRV (as estimate), BP series with
  conditions, steps, subjective ratings, lab results (only if the user wants), spirometry results.
- **Computed:** 7-day averages, BP series averages (day 1 dropped), trends vs baseline.
- **Weekly review question:** "Are weight and waist moving in the intended direction, and is any
  vital sign (BP, resting HR) drifting away from my baseline?" Monthly: "Is any check-up or lab
  recheck due?"

## Not medical advice — see a doctor if …

- home BP averages ≥ 135/85, or any reading ≥ 180/110 (with symptoms: call 112);
- resting HR repeatedly > 100 or < 40 (without endurance training), or palpitations/fainting;
- unexplained weight loss, night sweats, or fever with an abnormal blood count;
- persistent cough > 3 weeks, coughing blood, or worsening breathlessness.
