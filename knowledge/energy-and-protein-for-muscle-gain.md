# Energy and protein for muscle gain

For people who want to add muscle — including lean people who find it hard to gain weight.

## Energy balance in one paragraph

Body weight changes when energy in and energy out differ over weeks. Muscle can grow without a
surplus (especially for beginners), but a **small, steady surplus** makes gaining easier and is
the normal recommendation for lean people. [STRONG] Bigger surpluses mostly add fat, not more
muscle. [MODERATE — small randomised trial in trained lifters: 5 % vs 15 % surplus gave similar
muscle gains but more fat with 15 % (1)]

## How fast should weight go up?

| Situation | Typical target | Label |
|---|---|---|
| Novice / re-starter, lean | ~0.25–0.5 % of body weight **per week** (≈1–2 %/month), lower end if waist grows fast | [MODERATE] (2) |
| Several years of consistent training | lower end of that range or ~0.25–0.5 %/month | [OPINION] coaching practice (2) |
| Surplus size that usually achieves this | ~10–20 % above maintenance, or roughly +200–400 kcal/day | [MODERATE] (2) |

Example: 75 kg novice → about +0.2–0.35 kg/week. Returning lifters often regain lost muscle
quickly, so they can treat the first months like a novice. [MODERATE] (see strength page)

## Why the weight trend beats calorie counting

- Food labels and databases can be off by 10–20 %, and portion estimates more. [MODERATE]
- Daily weight swings of 0.5–2 kg come from water, salt, carbs and gut content. [STRONG]
- A **7-day average of morning weights**, compared week to week, shows the real direction.
  Adjust intake by ~150–250 kcal/day if the average misses the target for 2–3 weeks. [OPINION]
- Waist circumference rising much faster than weight suggests too large a surplus. [OPINION]

## Protein: how much

- General adult reference (DGE): **0.8 g/kg/day**; EFSA: 0.83 g/kg/day. That covers needs for
  health, not maximum muscle gain. [STRONG] (3, 4)
- For people doing resistance training: **1.6 g/kg/day** is where extra protein stops adding
  measurable muscle on average in the largest meta-analysis; **up to ~2.2 g/kg** covers people
  who respond more. [STRONG] (5)
- ISSN position: 1.4–2.0 g/kg/day for most exercising people. [STRONG] (6)
- Above ~2.2 g/kg there is no clear extra muscle benefit; it is not known to harm healthy kidneys
  but it crowds out other food and costs money. [MODERATE]

## Protein: how to spread it

- 3–5 meals with roughly **0.4 g/kg each** (≈25–40 g for most adults) is a practical pattern.
  [MODERATE] (2, 7)
- Total daily protein matters more than exact timing. [STRONG] (6)
- A protein-rich meal or snack before sleep is reasonable if the day is short. [WEAK]

## When eating enough is the hard part

All [OPINION] unless marked; these are common dietitian tactics.
- **Drink some calories:** milk, kefir, smoothies with oats, banana, peanut butter.
- **Energy-dense add-ons:** olive oil on vegetables, nuts, nut butter, cheese, dried fruit.
- **Eat on a schedule**, not on hunger: 3 meals + 2 snacks at fixed times.
- **Keep ready-to-eat options** (skyr, nuts, bread + cheese) where you are.
- **Bigger plates, less volume:** fewer very high-fibre, watery foods when the goal is surplus.
- Appetite often drops for 1–2 weeks after stopping regular cannabis use, while stopping nicotine
  usually raises appetite. Plan easy foods for those weeks. [MODERATE] (8)
- Many people find appetite rises after a few weeks of regular training. [WEAK]

## How body-os uses this

- **Tracked:** morning weight (7-day average), waist, meals with estimated kcal and protein
  (`bos food calc`), training log.
- **Estimated:** maintenance calories from weight trend vs logged intake over ≥ 2 weeks.
- **Weekly review question:** "Is the weekly average weight moving at the target rate, and is
  protein reaching ~1.6 g/kg on most days?"

## Not medical advice — see a doctor if …

- you lose weight without trying, or cannot gain despite eating clearly more;
- eating feels compulsive, restrictive, or causes distress;
- you have kidney disease, diabetes or another condition before raising protein a lot;
- you notice persistent digestive problems, fatigue or dizziness.
