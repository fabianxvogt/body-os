# Tobacco, nicotine and cannabis

Neutral information for people who use, cut down or quit. Your goals set the direction.

## Mixed joints: why the tobacco matters

- In Europe **~80–90 % of cannabis users mix it with tobacco**, mostly in joints. [MODERATE] (1)
- Tobacco brings **nicotine dependence**: some cravings for "a joint" are partly nicotine cravings.
  [MODERATE] (1)
- People who use both are more dependent on cannabis and have **worse cannabis-quitting
  outcomes** than cannabis-only users; effects on tobacco quitting are less consistent.
  [MODERATE] (2) Mixers were less likely to want help to cut down. [WEAK] (1)

## Harm-reduction options (if not quitting now)

- **Tobacco-free use** removes the nicotine exposure from joints; if you are nicotine-dependent,
  cover it with NRT rather than tobacco. [OPINION] consistent with (3)
- **Avoid combustion:** dry-herb vaporizers or edibles avoid smoke; edibles act slowly — start low,
  wait ≥ 2 h before more. Avoid deep inhalation and breath-holding. [MODERATE] (3)
- Pure cannabis joints can feel stronger per puff; smaller amounts may be enough. [OPINION]
- Fewer use days and later-in-the-day-only rules reduce dependence risk. [MODERATE] (3)

## Quit methods compared (tobacco) — relative chance of staying quit ≥ 6 months

| Method | Effect | Label |
|---|---|---|
| Unaided ("cold turkey") | ~3–5 % stay quit 6–12 months per attempt; most relapses in first week | [MODERATE] (4) |
| Any single NRT (patch, gum, lozenge, spray) | ×1.55 vs no NRT | [STRONG] (5) |
| **Combination NRT** (patch + fast form) | ×1.27 vs single NRT | [STRONG] (6) |
| **Varenicline** | ×2.32 vs placebo; ×1.25 vs single NRT | [STRONG] (7) |
| Cytisine | ×1.30 vs placebo; ≈ varenicline (×1.00, low certainty) | [MODERATE] (7) |
| Nicotine e-cigarettes | ×1.55 vs NRT (≈ +3 quitters per 100) | [STRONG] (8) |
| Individual counselling | ×1.57 vs minimal support; ×1.24 on top of NRT | [STRONG] (9) |
| Any extra behavioural support on top of medication | ×1.15 | [STRONG] (10) |
| Proactive quitline calls (for callers) | ×1.38 | [MODERATE] (11) |

A network meta-analysis found **e-cigarettes, varenicline and cytisine** most effective, closely
followed by combination NRT; adding behavioural support increases each. [STRONG] (12)
Long-term effects of vaping are not fully known; the aim is usually to stop vaping later too.
[MODERATE] (8)

## Withdrawal timelines

| | Nicotine | Cannabis |
|---|---|---|
| Starts | within hours | day 1–3 |
| Peak | days 1–3 (first week) | days 2–6 |
| Most symptoms gone | 2–4 weeks | 1–2 weeks (heavy use: 3–4+ weeks) |
| Typical symptoms | irritability, restlessness, poor focus, hunger, low mood, sleep disruption | irritability, anxiety, poor sleep, vivid dreams, low appetite, restlessness |
| Lingers | cue cravings for months; weight gain | sleep/dream changes |
| Labels | [MODERATE] (13) | [MODERATE] (14) |

## Relapse is data, not failure

- Most people need several attempts before quitting for good. [STRONG]
- After a lapse, log **when, where, mood, who, what you used**, and what would have helped; two or
  three lapses usually reveal the pattern. Restart quickly — one lapse does not erase progress. [OPINION]

## Triggers, habits and environment

- **If–then plans** ("If I finish dinner, then I go for a 10-min walk") roughly double quit odds in
  a meta-analysis of small studies. [WEAK–MODERATE] (15)
- **Environment design:** no tobacco/papers/grinder at home, change the routes and rituals linked
  to use, tell friends your plan. [OPINION]
- **Substitute the ritual:** something for hands and mouth (tea, gum), breathing exercises. [OPINION]
- **Exercise:** even 10–15 min of moderate activity cuts cravings in the moment. [STRONG] (16)
  Adding exercise programmes has not been shown to raise long-term quit rates. [MODERATE] (17)

## Germany: support options (checked October 2026)

- **Telephone quitline** of the Bundesinstitut für Öffentliche Gesundheit (BIÖG, formerly BZgA):
  0800 8 31 31 31, free; online programme rauchfrei-info.de. [STRONG — official] (18)
- **Cannabis:** free online programme "Quit the Shit" (BIÖG with local addiction services) via
  drugcom.de. [STRONG — official] (19)
- **Courses under §20 SGB V:** health insurers (GKV) subsidise smoking-cessation courses certified
  by the Zentrale Prüfstelle Prävention, usually after ≥ 80 % attendance; amounts differ by insurer.
  [MODERATE — check your insurer] (20)
- **Medication on prescription — new since 20 Aug 2025:** GKV pays for **nicotine replacement and
  varenicline** (not cytisine or bupropion) for people with **severe tobacco dependence** (e.g.
  Fagerström score ≥ 6, or failed quitting despite COPD, asthma, heart disease) who take part in
  an **evidence-based quit programme** (incl. listed DiGA apps). One course; review after 3 months;
  repeat no earlier than 3 years later. NRT combinations allowed if one is a patch; NRT plus
  varenicline not. [STRONG — G-BA decision] (21, 22)

## How body-os uses this

- **Tracked:** each use (substance, form, amount, time, context/trigger, mood), cravings (0–10),
  quit dates, lapses, NRT/medication use, withdrawal symptoms, money saved.
- **Weekly review questions:** "Is use moving toward my own goal? Which triggers came up most, and
  which substitute worked?" Sleep, appetite and training are reviewed alongside.

## Not medical advice — see a doctor if …

- you want medication support (varenicline, NRT on prescription) or are pregnant;
- low mood, anxiety or suicidal thoughts during quitting, or heavy use returns after psychosis-like
  symptoms (paranoia, hallucinations);
- **cyclic severe vomiting** with heavy cannabis use (hot showers bring relief) — possible
  cannabinoid hyperemesis syndrome;
- chest pain, coughing blood, or breathlessness.
