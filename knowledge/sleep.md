# Sleep

## How much and how regular

- Adults need **7 hours or more** per night on a regular basis; less is linked to worse health,
  mood and performance. Some people need 9+. [STRONG] (1)
- **Regularity** (similar sleep and wake times every day, including weekends) predicts health
  outcomes in large cohort data, possibly more strongly than duration. [MODERATE] (2)
- A fixed wake-up time is the single most useful anchor. [OPINION] consistent with CBT-I (3)

## Caffeine

- Half-life is typically ~3–6 h in adults but varies widely (genes, liver, hormones,
  contraceptives). **Smoking speeds caffeine breakdown**; after quitting smoking the same coffee
  gives higher caffeine levels — a common hidden cause of jitteriness and poor sleep in the first
  weeks. [MODERATE] (4)
- Meta-analysis: caffeine cut total sleep time by ~45 min and deep sleep by ~11 min on average.
  To avoid losing sleep, a regular coffee (~100 mg) should be taken **≥ ~9 h before bed**, a
  strong pre-workout dose (~200 mg) **≥ ~13 h**. [MODERATE] (5)
- EFSA: up to 400 mg/day is safe for healthy adults, but 100 mg close to bedtime can disturb
  sleep in some people. [STRONG] (6)

## Alcohol

- Helps some people fall asleep faster at high doses, but **delays and reduces REM sleep**,
  starting from ~2 standard drinks and getting worse with dose. Second-half-of-night waking is
  common. [STRONG] (7)

## Cannabis

- Polysomnography studies show **no consistent change** in sleep duration or stages with
  cannabis use; older small studies with high THC doses suggested REM suppression, newer ones
  are mixed. Many users *feel* they sleep better — subjective and measured sleep differ.
  [MODERATE] (8)
- **Stopping regular use** reliably causes sleep problems: harder falling asleep, shorter sleep,
  and **REM rebound** (very vivid or strange dreams). [MODERATE] (8, 9)
- Timeline: starts within 1–3 days, peaks around days 2–6, most symptoms settle in 1–2 weeks;
  sleep and dreams can stay disturbed for 3–4+ weeks in heavy users. [MODERATE] (9, 10)
- Withdrawal sleep problems are a frequent reason for relapse — knowing they are temporary
  helps. [MODERATE] (10)

## Nicotine

- Smokers on average take longer to fall asleep, sleep less, and have more light / less deep
  sleep than never-smokers; former smokers look like never-smokers. [MODERATE] (11)
- Nicotine is a stimulant; it also suppresses REM. Overnight withdrawal may contribute to early
  waking. [MODERATE] (12)
- In the first days after quitting, arousals and wake time increase; this improves over weeks.
  [MODERATE] (12)
- Nicotine patches worn overnight can cause vivid dreams; removing at bedtime is an option
  (ask a pharmacist). [MODERATE]

## Wearables and sleep apps

- Good at detecting *sleep vs not sleep* (sensitivity ≥ 0.93 in a lab comparison) but **poor at
  detecting wake** while lying still (specificity ~0.2–0.5), so they often overestimate sleep.
  [MODERATE] (13)
- **Sleep stages (deep, REM, light) are estimates**, with only moderate agreement with lab
  polysomnography; accuracy drops on bad nights. Use trends, not single-night stage numbers.
  [MODERATE] (13)
- A simple sleep diary (bedtime, wake time, estimated time to fall asleep, awakenings, quality
  1–5) remains the clinical standard for insomnia. [STRONG] (3)

## Insomnia: CBT-I first

- **Cognitive behavioural therapy for insomnia (CBT-I)** is the recommended first-line treatment
  for chronic insomnia in adults, in person or digital; sleep pills are a second step and
  short-term. [STRONG] (3, 14)
- Core parts: fixed wake time, bed only for sleep, get up after ~20 min awake, temporary
  sleep-window restriction, working on worry about sleep. [STRONG] (14)
- In Germany, digital CBT-I programmes exist as prescribable DiGA (e.g. somnio). [MODERATE —
  check current DiGA directory]

## Practical levers (easiest first)

1. Same wake time daily; morning daylight outside for 10–30 min. [MODERATE]
2. Caffeine cut-off ~9 h before bed. [MODERATE] (5)
3. Last alcohol ≥ 3 h before bed, ideally fewer drinks. [MODERATE] (7)
4. Cool, dark, quiet bedroom; phone out of reach. [OPINION]
5. Wind-down routine 30–60 min; no heavy meals right before bed. [OPINION]
6. Exercise regularly, but very hard sessions late at night may delay sleep for some. [MODERATE]

## How body-os uses this

- **Tracked:** bed/wake times, sleep quality 1–5, wearable totals (marked as estimates), caffeine
  and alcohol with times, cannabis/nicotine use, naps.
- **Computed:** average duration, regularity (spread of wake times), last-caffeine-to-bed gap.
- **Weekly review question:** "Was my sleep long and regular enough, and what changed on the bad
  nights (late caffeine, alcohol, substance changes, stress)?" Correlations are hypotheses.

## Not medical advice — see a doctor if …

- loud snoring with breathing pauses, or daytime sleepiness that risks driving safety;
- insomnia ≥ 3 nights/week for ≥ 3 months, or affecting daily life;
- low mood, anxiety or thoughts of self-harm with sleep loss;
- severe withdrawal symptoms (confusion, hallucinations, seizures — especially with alcohol).
